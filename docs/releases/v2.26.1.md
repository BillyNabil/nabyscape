# NabyScape 2.26.1 — notifications on the dock

## Alerts appear on the dock

- A new alert now grows out of the closed dock as a compact banner: the sender's icon, its name and time, the title and one line of text. It folds back on its own after the **Notification lifetime** (4–12 seconds), stays while the pointer is over it, and alerts that arrive together follow one after another.
- Click the banner to open the sending app, or the full card for NabyScape alerts. The **›** button opens the notification center.
- A short accent line under the closed dock shows that unread alerts are waiting in the center.
- Banners wait while the panel is open or you are dragging or editing, then appear when it closes.

## Notification center

- Rows show the sender and time, the title in bold and two lines of text. Click a row to open the app (or the folder, or the update page); the remove button appears on hover.
- Round sender icons: the app's own logo for other apps, Naby for NabyScape's alerts, and a coloured initial when Windows provides no logo.
- The center follows **Panel width** and stays within **Maximum panel height**; its header and buttons stay in place while the list scrolls, so the bottom is never cut off. Banners and alert cards follow **Hover width**.

## Test notifications and quiet hours

- **Send test notification** in Settings → Notifications, the tray menu or the notification center shows exactly how an alert appears, even during quiet hours.
- **Show during quiet hours** (Settings → Notifications → Other apps) lets alerts from other apps appear while quiet hours are on. It is off by default.

## Fixes

- A second queued alert could expire before its turn; each alert now gets its full time on screen when it reaches the front.

## Download and update

Use x64 Setup for Intel/AMD PCs or ARM64 Setup for Windows on ARM. Both target Windows 10 build 19041+ and Windows 11; the .NET runtime is bundled. Existing preferences, models and personal content are preserved. Portable packages, dependency notices and SHA-256 checksums accompany the installers. Installers are unsigned. Physical Windows 10 and native ARM64 execution are not validated; ARM64 is cross-compiled and structurally checked.
